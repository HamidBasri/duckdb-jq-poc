Five words to start with

Table
One input file, CSV or Parquet. There are five.
Row
One thing in a table: one trade, one company, one instrument.
Column
One piece of information about every row, like price. Every column arrives as text, even numbers and dates.
Key
The column that names a row, so other rows can find it. In trades it is trade_id.
Link
A column that holds another row's key. A trade's book_id is a link to a row in books.

The tables and their links

trades sits in the middle and links to the other four. Three links point back into their own table, which makes chains: a trade's earlier version, a company's parent, an instrument's underlying.

These are the same kinds of mess the data generator adds on purpose to a share of rows. Every one of them is dealt with in step 3 or 4.

What to remember

  • Five tables come in. Only trades becomes output documents; the other four are looked up through links.
  • A key names a row. A link is a column holding another row's key.
  • Three links point back into their own table and make chains. Those chains are the hardest part, and they come back in step 3.
  • Every column arrives as text, mess included.
